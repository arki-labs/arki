## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
1. Symbolic `derivative` of any order over the expr function set. A separate `derivation` entry point returns the full rule-by-rule derivation tree, with stable rule ids and i18n template keys (§9). Callers who do not want steps pay nothing for them.
2. `expand`, `collect`, `rationalize` and polynomial extraction, exactly, with coefficients in ℚ[π, e] (§5.3). `gcd` of polynomials. **Complete factoring of univariate polynomials over ℚ** (§8.2).
3. A small, documented `simplify`: a fixed rule table, each rule with its stated condition (§7).
4. Truthful results. Every condition a rewrite needs is returned with the result. What cas cannot do stays unevaluated or throws a typed error. It never guesses (§6).
5. Input and output are the expr AST, so results print, render as LaTeX and compile with expr (§5).
6. Bounded: every operation has budgets checked before the work, and cancellation (§12).
7. Zero runtime dependencies beyond `@arki/expr` and `@arki/math`. Runs on Node, Bun, Deno, browsers and Hermes (§16).
8. A general-purpose library: designed for public use, with Ekuation as the first acceptance test.

**Non-goals** (synthesis "Never" list, plus the scope that follows from §19)
- Symbolic integration (Risch or heuristic), symbolic limits, series, general equation solving. `exactRoots` (§8.3) only reads off closed forms for factors of degree ≤ 2; it is not a solver.
- SymPy or Mathematica parity. Trig-identity heuristics (`sin^2 + cos^2 → 1`), log expansion, radical denesting.
- A complex domain. v1 is real-only (§6).
- Full multivariate factoring (Wang's algorithm). Multivariate and ℚ[π, e]-coefficient input gets content + square-free + gcd only, marked `complete: false` (§8.2).
- Constants other than π and e as coefficients (`sqrt(2)*x`, `ln(2)*x`).
- User-defined rewrite rules (mathjs accepts rule strings: a second parser surface).
- A "try everything, keep the shortest" simplifier. Output form is a normal form, not a search result.

## 4. Design overview

```
expr AST ──read──▶ term DAG (hash-consed, kernels) ──▶ normal forms (Poly / RatFun over ℚ[π, e])
                                                         │   rules with conditions
expr AST ◀──parse(print)── canonical tree ◀──────────────┘   + conditions (+ derivation tree, opt-in)
```

All work happens on an internal representation. The public API takes and returns expr values only.

## 5. Representation

### 5.1 Input

An `Expr` (`version: 1`) or a string, which is parsed with expr's canonical `scientific` syntax. Locale input is the caller's job: parse with `syntaxFor(locale)` first, then pass the `Expr`.
- `group` nodes are transparent. `implicit` is ignored (it only affects printing).
- Number literals are read from `raw` with expr's Rational reader, so `0.01` is exactly `1/100` and `1e-7` is `1/10^7`.
- Names resolve through `names(expr, { language })`: variables, the constants `pi` and `e`, and functions. `i` is rejected in v1 (`CasError` `not-in-domain`, with its span).
- Host functions from a custom `Language` are opaque: `simplify` treats `f(u)` as a kernel; `derivative` throws `unsupported-function` if `u` contains the variable, and returns `0` if it does not.
- cas accepts AST `version: 1` only. cas and expr are both 0.x and move in lockstep; there is no shim.

### 5.2 Internal forms

- **Term DAG.** Nodes are hash-consed per call: equal subterms are one object. The DAG is built for one call and dropped afterwards; it is never a cache across calls.
- **Kernels.** Anything that is not `+ - * / ^integer` over names, π, e and rationals: `sin(u)`, `exp(u)`, `ln(u)`, `u^(p/q)`, `abs(u)`, opaque host calls. Kernels are keyed by their canonical print. Their arguments are normalized recursively.
- **`Poly`.** A sparse multivariate polynomial over `Rational`: a map from monomial (sorted exponent vector over names, the generators π and e, and kernels) to a non-zero coefficient. Univariate dense arrays over ℤ and ℤ/pℤ are used inside `gcd` and `factor`.
- **`RatFun`.** A numerator and denominator `Poly`, with a gcd-cancelled, sign-normalized denominator (positive leading coefficient, integer content). Every cancelled factor becomes a condition (§6).

### 5.3 The coefficient domain: ℚ[π, e]

- π and e are **generators**: the coefficients of a polynomial in `x` are elements of ℚ[π, e], polynomials in π and e with rational coefficients. Division by a non-zero constant stays exact in the fraction field ℚ(π, e), stored as a reduced fraction of two ℚ[π, e] polynomials.
- **`e` the constant is not `exp`.** `e` standing alone, or raised to an integer (`e^2`, `e^-1`), is the generator e. `e^u` with `u` not an integer literal is the kernel `exp(u)`, the same kernel as `exp(u)`: `e^x`, `e^(2x)`, and also `e^(1/2)`. So `e^2*x` has the coefficient `e^2`, and `e^x` is never a coefficient.
- **Zero testing assumes π and e are algebraically independent.** An element of ℚ[π, e] is taken to be zero exactly when all its rational coefficients are zero. That assumption is **not a theorem**. Whether π and e are algebraically independent is an open problem; it is not even known whether `π + e` or `π·e` is irrational. Schanuel's conjecture would imply independence, and no relation is known. Treating π and e as free generators is the standard CAS convention: SymPy builds the polynomial domain `QQ[pi,E]` with them as free generators, and Mathematica's and Maple's rational normal forms (`Together`, `normal`) treat `Pi` and `E` as independent symbols too. The README states the assumption next to every function that zero-tests.
- **Signs of constants** (needed for discriminants and the sign oracle) are decided numerically and rigorously: evaluate with outward-rounded float intervals around π and e; if the interval contains 0, re-evaluate with `@arki/math` `Decimal` (`PI(scale)`, `exp(1, { scale })`) at doubling precision, up to the `digits` budget. If the sign is still unknown, the answer is "unknown", never a guess. A constant that is identically zero in ℚ[π, e] is caught by the zero test first, so the loop only meets non-zero values.
- Other constant kernels (`sqrt(2)`, `ln(2)`, `sin(1)`) are not coefficients. `polynomialIn` reports them as `other` (`unsupported-constant`), and EQ goes numeric.

### 5.4 Output and canonical form (decisions 2 and 3)

cas builds a canonical tree, prints it with expr's `print`, and parses that text again. So a result is a normal `Expr`: `source` is canonical text, spans point into it, `parse(print(r))` holds by construction, and `toLatex` works unchanged. cas emits no `group` nodes; expr's printer adds parentheses where the tree needs them. The re-parse costs one parse per result. It buys one code path for spans and a round-trip guarantee.

- Sums: by descending degree in the main variable (the differentiation or `collect` variable, else the first name in source order), then kernels in a fixed order, constant last: `3*x^2 - 4*x + 1`.
- Products: the coefficient first (rational, then π, then e), then names, then kernels: `2*pi*x`, `2*x*y` when `x` is the main variable.
- Rational functions: one fraction. The numerator is expanded. The denominator is kept as a product of the factors it was built from: `(x^2 - 2*x - 1)/(x - 1)^2`.
- A common non-polynomial factor of all terms is pulled out: `exp(x)*(cos(x) - sin(x))`, not `exp(x)*cos(x) - exp(x)*sin(x)`.
- Negative terms print as subtraction.
- **Numbers.** `numbers: 'fraction'` (default) prints exact fractions: `x/50 + 5`. `numbers: 'decimal'` prints a rational as a decimal **only when it terminates** (`0.02*x + 5`); `1/3` stays `1/3` in either mode. The value is identical; only the text differs. Ekuation passes `'decimal'` when the input used decimal literals.

## 6. Assumptions and conditions (decision 4)

v1 is **real-only**: every variable ranges over ℝ, and every function means its real version.

**The contract.** Let D be the input's natural domain: every denominator ≠ 0, `ln`/`log` argument > 0, `log(x, b)` with b > 0 and b ≠ 1, `sqrt` and even-root arguments ≥ 0, `asin`/`acos` with 1 − u² ≥ 0, `tan`/`sec` with `cos(u)` ≠ 0, and so on. Then:

1. Wherever the input is defined and the caller's assumptions hold, the result is defined and has the same value.
2. A rewrite may **widen** the domain (remove a point where the input was undefined). Every such lost restriction is returned in `conditions`.
3. A rewrite may **never narrow** it (change the value somewhere the input is defined) unless the caller's assumptions prove the rewrite safe. Without proof the rewrite is not applied and the term stays as it is.
4. **Every condition a rewrite needs is returned.** That includes caller assumptions a rewrite relied on (reason `assumed`). A result never depends on something it does not list.

```ts
type Relation = 'nonzero' | 'positive' | 'nonnegative';
type Condition = {
  readonly subject: Expr;               // canonical, e.g. `x - 1`
  readonly relation: Relation;
  readonly reason: 'domain' | 'differentiable' | 'assumed';
};
```

**The required examples.**

| Rewrite | When applied | Result carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `x/x → 1` | always | `x ≠ 0` (lost restriction) |
| `(x^2 - 1)/(x - 1) → x + 1` | always | `x - 1 ≠ 0` |
| `sqrt(x^2) → abs(x)` | always (real x) | nothing |
| `sqrt(x^2) → x` | only if `x ≥ 0` is assumed or proven | `x ≥ 0` (`assumed`) when it came from the caller |
| `sqrt(x)^2 → x` | always | `x ≥ 0` |
| `exp(ln(x)) → x` | always | `x > 0` |
| `ln(exp(x)) → x` | always | nothing: over ℝ this holds for **every** x. (Over ℂ it fails outside the principal strip; that is why the domain is explicit.) |
| `(x^a)^b → x^(a*b)`, non-integer `b` | only if `x > 0` is assumed or proven | `x > 0` (`assumed`) when it came from the caller |
| `0 * ln(x) → 0` | always | `x > 0` |

**Supplying assumptions**: per call, per variable.

```ts
type Assumption = 'nonzero' | 'positive' | 'nonnegative' | 'negative';
type Assumptions = Readonly<Record<string, Assumption>>;
```

**The sign oracle** decides "proven". It is small, sound and incomplete, and documented as a table: assumed signs of names; signs of ℚ[π, e] constants (§5.3); `exp(u)` > 0; `u^2k` ≥ 0; `abs(u)` ≥ 0; sums of non-negatives with one positive term > 0 (`x^2 + 1`); products and quotients by sign rules. When it cannot decide, the answer is "unknown", and unknown never licenses a rewrite. A fact the oracle proves from the expression itself (`x^2 + 1 > 0`) is not a condition: it holds everywhere.

**Pruning.** A lost restriction is not listed when the result still shows it (the same subject is still a denominator, a `ln` argument, and so on): the result is undefined there anyway. Matching is exact on canonical subjects, and `positive` implies `nonzero` and `nonnegative`. Conditions are deduplicated.

**Derivatives** carry lost domain restrictions (d/dx `ln(x)` = `1/x` with `x > 0`, reason `domain`) and points where the input is not differentiable (d/dx `abs(x)` = `sign(x)` with `x ≠ 0`, reason `differentiable`).

`conditionToLatex(c)` in the core prints `x \neq 0`, `x > 0`, `x \geq 0`. It is math notation, not English, so apps need no translation for it.

## 7. `simplify`: the rule table

`simplify` = build the `RatFun` normal form (phases `fold`, `expand`, `collect`, `cancel`) + the kernel rules below, applied bottom-up to a fixed point. Each rule only shrinks or reorders the term under a well-founded order, so the fixed point exists; the fuel budget (§12) is a backstop, not the termination argument.

| Id | Rewrite | Condition needed | Recorded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `fold` | exact ℚ arithmetic on literals; `0*u → 0`; `u + 0`, `1*u`, `u^1`, `u/1` | — | lost restrictions of dropped `u` |
| `expand` | products and integer powers of sums (inside the normal form) | — | — |
| `collect` | like terms and like powers (`x + x → 2*x`, `x*x → x^2`) | — | — |
| `cancel` | gcd cancellation in a fraction | — | cancelled factor ≠ 0 |
| `integer-powers` | `u^a * u^b → u^(a+b)`, `(u^a)^b → u^(a*b)` for integers | — | `u ≠ 0` when a negative exponent disappears |
| `fractional-powers` | the same for non-integer exponents | `u > 0` assumed or proven | the assumption |
| `sqrt-of-square` | `sqrt(u^2) → abs(u)`; `→ u` | `u ≥ 0` assumed or proven for `→ u` | the assumption |
| `square-of-sqrt` | `sqrt(u)^2 → u`, likewise `nthRoot` | — | `u ≥ 0` |
| `exp-ln` | `exp(ln(u)) → u` | — | `u > 0` |
| `ln-exp` | `ln(exp(u)) → u` | real (v1 always) | — |
| `exp-product` | `exp(a) * exp(b) → exp(a + b)`, `exp(a)^n → exp(n*a)` | — | — |
| `abs-sign` | `abs(u) → u` or `-u`; `abs(u)/u → sign(u)` | sign of `u` assumed or proven for the first; — for the second | the assumption; `u ≠ 0` for the second |
| `parity` | `sin(-u) → -sin(u)`, `cos(-u) → cos(u)`, and the other odd and even functions | — | — |
| `special-values` | `sin(0)`, `cos(0)`, `tan(0)`, `exp(0)`, `ln(1)`, `ln(e)`, `sqrt` and `abs` of rationals with exact results (`sqrt(4/9) → 2/3`) | — | — |
| `factor-out` | pull a common non-polynomial factor out of a sum (§5.4) | — | — |

`simplify` returns the ids it applied. The same ids label simplification steps in a derivation (§9). Anything not in this table is left alone. The table is the documentation; adding a rule is a minor release with a changelog entry and its own property tests.

## 8. Polynomial operations

### 8.1 Normal forms and extraction

- **`expand(e)`**: products and integer powers of sums, by the multinomial theorem. Kernels stay as atoms.
- **`collect(e, x)`**: group by powers of `x`; coefficients may contain other names, π, e and kernels.
- **`rationalize(e)`**: one fraction `numerator/denominator` with polynomial parts, cancelled by gcd, with conditions.
- **`polynomialIn(e, x)`**: the EQ entry point. It answers a question, so it returns a union instead of throwing:
  - `polynomial`: ascending coefficients in ℚ(π, e) (`[]` for the zero polynomial), plus conditions (`x/x - 1` → `[]` with `x ≠ 0`).
  - `rational`: numerator and denominator coefficients after cancellation; the roots of the denominator are excluded.
  - `other`: reason `transcendental` (a kernel contains `x`), `unsupported-constant` (§5.3) or `other-variable` (another free name), with the span of the first offending input node.
- **`gcd(a, b)`**: univariate over ℚ by primitive subresultant PRS over ℤ after clearing denominators, result monic. Multivariate (which includes ℚ[π, e] coefficients, with π and e as variables) is recursive: one main variable, coefficients in ℚ[the rest]. A modular (Brown) gcd is added later only if benchmarks show the need.

### 8.2 `factor` (decision 7)

**Complete for univariate polynomials with rational coefficients.** The algorithm is Zassenhaus's, as in every serious CAS:

1. **Content and primitive part.** Clear denominators (lcm), take the integer content. `f = c · g`, `g` primitive in ℤ[x] with a positive leading coefficient.
2. **Square-free factorization** (Yun's algorithm): `g = ∏ gᵢ^i` with each `gᵢ` square-free. Steps 3–6 run on each `gᵢ`. Linear and constant parts finish here.
3. **Choose a prime.** Small primes `p` that do not divide the leading coefficient and keep `gᵢ mod p` square-free. Try up to 5 such primes and keep the one with the fewest modular factors, since recombination cost grows with that count.
4. **Factor mod p.** Distinct-degree factorization, then Cantor–Zassenhaus equal-degree splitting. The randomness comes from a fixed-seed PRNG, so a run is reproducible. (Berlekamp is the alternative for very small `p`; one of the two is picked in the spike, by benchmark.)
5. **Hensel lifting.** Lift the modular factorization (multifactor, via a factor tree, quadratic lifting) to `p^k > 2·lc(g)·B`, where `B` is the Landau–Mignotte bound on the coefficients of any factor of `g`: for a factor of degree `d`, `|coefficient| ≤ binomial(d, j)·‖g‖₂`, with `B` the largest such value.
6. **Recombination.** Try products of lifted factors, subsets of size 1, 2, …, up to half the factors, with symmetric representation mod `p^k`. A candidate is a true factor when its primitive part divides `g` over ℤ (early-abort trial division, constant-term test first). Factors found are removed and the search continues on the cofactor.

Recombination is exponential in the number of modular factors in the worst case (Swinnerton-Dyer polynomials split into many factors mod every prime). The `recombination` budget (§12) bounds it, and breaking it throws `CasLimitError`; cas never returns a factorization labelled complete that it has not proven. **van Hoeij's algorithm** (lattice reduction on traces of the lifted factors) removes that blow-up; it is the planned later speedup if benchmarks or users hit the budget.

**Scope that follows from the decision.** For multivariate input, and for univariate input whose coefficients involve π or e (which is multivariate once π and e are generators), v1 does content, square-free factorization and gcd only, and returns `complete: false`. Full multivariate factoring (Wang's algorithm) is out of v1.

```ts
type Factored = {
  readonly content: Expr;                                  // a rational, or a ℚ[π, e] constant
  readonly factors: readonly { readonly factor: Expr; readonly multiplicity: number }[];
  readonly complete: boolean;                               // true: every factor is irreducible over ℚ
};
```

### 8.3 `exactRoots`: closed forms for small factors (decision 6)

`exactRoots(form)` takes a `polynomial` result of `polynomialIn` and returns what has a closed form:

| Input | Exact result | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rational coefficients | `factor` over ℚ; every linear factor gives a rational root, every quadratic factor gives `(-b ± sqrt(Δ))/(2a)` with `Δ` simplified (`sqrt(8) → 2*sqrt(2)`) | irreducible factors of degree ≥ 3 are returned as `numeric` |
| ℚ[π, e] coefficients, degree 1 | `-c0/c1` (`pi*x = 1` → `x = 1/pi`) | — |
| ℚ[π, e] coefficients, degree 2 | the quadratic formula with `Δ` in ℚ[π, e]; real or complex is decided by the sign of `Δ` (§5.3); a zero `Δ` gives a double root | — |
| ℚ[π, e] coefficients, degree ≥ 3 | content and square-free split only | each part returned as `numeric` |

`numeric` parts carry float coefficients for `@arki/math/polynomial` `polynomialRoots`, and the result says `exact: false` for them, so the app can label those roots as approximations. Cubic and quartic radical formulas are out (§17): they are rarely readable, and the numeric roots are better.

## 9. `derivative` and the derivation tree (decision 1)

### 9.1 Differentiation

- Rules: constant, variable, sum, constant multiple, product, quotient, power (constant exponent), exponential (constant base, including `e^u`), general power `u^v` via `exp(v*ln(u))`, logarithm, the trig, inverse trig, hyperbolic and inverse hyperbolic functions, roots, `abs`, and the chain rule. π and e are constants.
- The derivative is computed on the DAG with one derivative per shared subterm (memoized per call). So the cost is linear in the DAG, and a product rule cannot double the work.
- Higher orders normalize between orders, as DE does today (DE 539).
- `floor ceil round fix sign mod max min`, `factorial` and `gamma` throw `not-differentiable` or `unsupported-function` with the call's span (S-03). Their derivative is 0 almost everywhere; returning 0 would hide the jumps.

### 9.2 Two entry points: steps cost nothing unless asked for

- `@arki/cas/derivative` exports `derivative()`: result and conditions only.
- `@arki/cas/derivation` exports `derivation()`: the same result plus the derivation tree.
- Both call one internal differentiator that takes an optional recorder. `derivative` passes none, so each rule application costs one null check, and none of the tree, template or step-printing code is imported: tree-shaking removes it from `derivative`'s bundle (enforced by size-limit, §14). The benchmark runs `derivative` against a build of the differentiator with the recorder hook deleted, and the gap must stay under 3 %.
- Results are identical: the property tests check `derivation(e).result` equals `derivative(e).result` for every generated input.

### 9.3 The tree

Every rule application at every level is a step:

```ts
type DerivativeRule =
  | 'constant' | 'variable' | 'sum' | 'constant-multiple' | 'product' | 'quotient'
  | 'power' | 'exponential' | 'general-power' | 'logarithm' | 'function' | 'root'
  | 'absolute-value' | 'chain';
type StepRule =
  | { readonly kind: 'derivative'; readonly id: DerivativeRule; readonly fn?: string }  // fn: 'sin', 'atanh', …
  | { readonly kind: 'simplify'; readonly id: SimplifyRule }
  | { readonly kind: 'order'; readonly order: number }
  | { readonly kind: 'repeat'; readonly of: number };    // a shared subterm already derived in step `of`

type StepTemplate = {
  readonly key: string;                                   // e.g. 'derivative.product', 'derivative.function.sin', 'simplify.cancel'
  readonly params: Readonly<Record<string, Expr | number | string>>;  // e.g. { f: Expr, g: Expr }; strings only for names
};

type DerivationStep = {
  readonly index: number;                                 // pre-order position, stable within one result
  readonly rule: StepRule;
  readonly template: StepTemplate;
  readonly before: Expr;                                  // the operand; for derivative rules: the function u being differentiated
  readonly after: Expr;                                   // u′ for derivative rules; the rewritten expression for simplify rules
  readonly children: readonly DerivationStep[];
  readonly conditions?: readonly Condition[];             // conditions this step introduced
};

type Derivation = CasResult & { readonly steps: DerivationStep };   // the root
```

**Shape.** The root has one `order` child per order. Each `order` step has two children: the differentiation tree and a `simplify` step.
- In the differentiation tree, a node's children are the derivatives its rule needs. The product rule on `f·g` has children `f′` and `g′`. The **chain rule is its own step**: `sin(x^2)` is `chain` with children `function` (`fn: 'sin'`, `sin(u) → cos(u)`) and `power` (`x^2 → 2*x`). So the top chip for `sin(x^2)` is "chain rule", and `2^x` is `exponential`, which fixes both chips in §1.
- `before`/`after` of a derivative step are the operand and its derivative, with `fold` applied only. `after` of a parent is assembled from its children's `after`s.
- A subterm that appears twice is derived once. The second occurrence is a leaf with rule `repeat` pointing at the first. So the tree is linear in the DAG, not in the unfolded tree.
- The `simplify` step's children are the phases and rules of §7 in the order applied, each with whole-expression `before`/`after` and the conditions it added. The normal form is computed algebraically, not by rewriting, so `expand`, `collect` and `cancel` are shown as one step each, not as atomic rewrites. A phase that changes nothing is omitted.

**i18n.** No English is baked in. The template key is the translation key and `params` are values the app renders (`Expr` via `toLatex`). `ruleFormula(rule)` gives the language-neutral LaTeX formula (`(fg)' = f'g + fg'`). The README ships an English reference table of keys for apps to copy into their locale files; it is documentation, not code. The set of keys is exported as a frozen array, so an app test can assert every key is translated.

**Bounds** (§12). Before differentiating each order, cas knows the DAG size of the expression it is about to differentiate (the input, or the previous order's normalized result). The tree has at most `2 × DAG nodes` derivative steps (one rule step plus one possible `chain` step per node) and at most 16 simplify steps per order, so the `steps` budget is checked **before** each order starts. Step `Expr`s are printed once per DAG node and shared between steps; the total printed size is estimated from the DAG and checked against `stepNodes` before any step is emitted.

**As built (step 3, 2026-10-15).** Three differences from the sketch above:
- `StepRule` has three more container kinds: `derivation` (the root), `simplification` (an order's simplify step) and `normal-form`. The engine computes `fold`, `expand`, `collect` and `cancel` together in one algebraic pass, so they are one `normal-form` step whose `phases` list which of them happened; showing them separately would mean printing intermediate forms the engine never builds. The kernel rules are separate steps: the simplifier can switch rules on one at a time, and each rule that changes the text is shown in table order, then `factor-out`. A rule's step shows what switching it on changed.
- `repeat` is a separate `kind` (`{ kind: 'repeat', of }`), as sketched; repeated `constant` and `variable` leaves are shown again rather than as `repeat`.
- The `stepNodes` count is taken after the simplification stages run and before any step is printed. The stages print their own results, each one bounded by `nodes`.

**Ekuation's panel** renders the tree as nested, collapsible cards: the chip comes from `rule`, the explanation from the app's locale files by `template.key`, the math from `toLatex(before)` → `toLatex(after)`. Leaf steps (`constant`, `variable`, `repeat`) start collapsed. The header step (Leibniz notation of the input) stays Ekuation's.

  | { readonly kind: 'rational'; readonly numerator: readonly Coefficient[]; readonly denominator: readonly Coefficient[];

Coefficient arrays are ascending, like mathjs's, so EQ's indexing (EQ 373–404, 791–960) does not change.

## 11. Errors

| Error | Thrown by | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CasError extends MathError` | any verb | `code`, `remediation`, `span?` (into the input's `source`) |
| `CasLimitError extends ResourceLimitError` | any verb | `budget`, `limit`, `actual` |
| `ParseError`, `ExprLimitError` | string input, via expr | as in expr |
| `signal.reason` | any verb with a `signal` | whatever the host aborted with |

`CasError.code` follows `ParseError`'s pattern (a string union, not `ARKI_…`): `unsupported-function`, `not-differentiable`, `not-in-domain` (`i`, or a function cas has no real meaning for), `bad-variable` (the variable is a constant or function name; replaces DE 473–491), `not-rational` (`rationalize` on a transcendental input). Messages are English; apps translate by `code`.

## 12. Budgets and cancellation

Checked before the expensive work. Unlike expr, rewriting has no static cost bound, so a fuel counter backs the static checks.

| Budget | Default | Checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nodes` | 5 000 | on input; output tree size computed from the DAG (cheap count) before printing |
| `depth` | 256 | on input; internal algorithms are iterative |
| `terms` | 10 000 | before `expand`: multinomial count C(n+k−1, k−1) for `(k terms)^n`, products of term counts |
| `degree` | 1 000 per variable; 64 for `gcd` | before powers, expansion and gcd |
| `digits` | 1 000 per coefficient | before multiply (sum of digits), before power (n × digits), PRS growth bound before gcd; also the precision cap for π/e sign decisions |
| `order` | 10 | before `derivative`/`derivation` starts |
| `steps` | 2 000 derivation steps | before each order: 2 × DAG nodes + 16 |
| `stepNodes` | 50 000 printed nodes across all steps | estimated from the DAG before any step is emitted |
| `factorDegree` | 128 | before `factor`, on each square-free part |
| `factorDigits` | 300 digits in the largest coefficient; 2 000 in `p^k` | before factoring; the Landau–Mignotte bound fixes `k` before lifting |
| `recombination` | 65 536 candidate products | counted during recombination |
| `fuel` | 100 000 rewrite and arithmetic steps per call | counted; the `signal` is checked every 1 024 units, between orders and between Hensel steps |

Expression swell is the main risk: repeated quotient and product rules, `(x + y + 1)^n`. Hash-consing keeps derivatives linear in the DAG, and normalization between orders stops growth from compounding. The output size check turns what is left into a `CasLimitError`, never a hang.

## 13. Testing and correctness evidence

- **Property tests (fast-check, dev-only).**
  - `simplify`, `expand`, `rationalize`: on generated rational-function trees (`+ - * / ^int`, small literals, 1–3 names), `evaluate(result)` equals `evaluate(input)` **exactly** in `@arki/expr/rational`, at random rational points where the input is defined and the conditions hold. Where the input is defined, the result must be defined too.
  - Kernel rules: kernels are replaced consistently by fresh variables, so algebraic rules are tested exactly. Rules that are about kernels (`exp-ln`, `parity`, …) are tested in float at random points within 1e-12 relative.
  - **Conditions are necessary.** For each condition-bearing rule, a generated point that breaks the condition must show the input undefined (or different). A condition that never matters is a bug too.
  - **ℚ[π, e].** π and e are substituted by random rationals: every identity cas derives in ℚ[π, e] must then hold exactly in ℚ (a polynomial identity in free generators holds for every substitution). Constant signs are checked against `Decimal` at 200 digits.
  - `derivative`: polynomial inputs match `polynomialDerivative` of the coefficients exactly. All inputs match `@arki/math/differentiate` `derivative(f, x)` at random points within its error estimate. Linearity and the product rule hold as `simplify(lhs - rhs) = 0` on rational inputs.
  - **Derivation tree.** Every derivative step is checked on its own: `after` matches the numeric derivative of `before` at random points. Every parent's `after` is the rule's formula applied to its children's `after`s. The root's final `after` equals `derivative()`'s result. `derivation(e).result` equals `derivative(e).result`. Every emitted `template.key` is in `TEMPLATE_KEYS`.
  - `gcd` divides both inputs; `gcd · lcm = a · b` up to a unit.
  - `factor`: `expand(content · ∏ factorᵢ^mᵢ)` equals the input exactly; with `complete: true`, each factor is irreducible (checked against SymPy fixtures and by degree arguments mod several primes); products of random irreducibles factor back into exactly those factors.
  - `exactRoots`: every exact root, substituted back, gives exactly 0 in ℚ[π, e] with `sqrt` handled by squaring out.
  - `print`/`parse` round trip of every result and step (it holds by construction; the test keeps it true).
- **Oracle fixtures.** SymPy generates derivatives, gcds and factorizations for a fixed set, offline and dev-only, committed as JSON (the Wave 1 mpmath pattern). The factor set includes cyclotomic polynomials (`x^60 - 1`), Swinnerton-Dyer polynomials (recombination stress, budget expected to hold for `S_4`, degree 16), and Mignotte-style polynomials with large factor coefficients. No runtime dependency.
- **Differential test vs mathjs** on the Ekuation corpus: values at sample points within 1e-9 relative, or both fail, or the case is on the intended-difference list (§15).
- **Fuzzing** with random and grammar-aware ASTs, hostile cases included (`(x+1)^10000`, `x^(10^9)`, deep nesting, 5th derivative of `exp(x^x)` with steps, degree-128 factoring with 300-digit coefficients): only `CasError`, `CasLimitError`, expr's errors or math's errors may escape, and each input finishes within 100 ms (1 s for `factor`).
- **Security.** No `eval`, no `new Function`; rule tables are frozen data; names live in `Map`s, so `constructor` and `__proto__` are ordinary variables (tested); tampered AST JSON arrives through expr's `fromJSON`.
- **Acceptance:** one test per S-01–S-13.

## 19. Decisions (founder, 2026-10-15)

1. **Steps: the full rule-by-rule derivation tree** (option C). Every rule application at every level is a step with a stable rule id, an i18n template key and `before`/`after`/`children`/`conditions`; simplification phases are steps too. Steps live in a separate entry point, `@arki/cas/derivation`, so `derivative` callers pay nothing in size or speed (§9).
2. **Output: the readable normal form** (option B). Expanded polynomial part, one fraction over a factored denominator, common non-polynomial factors pulled out (§5.4).
3. **Numbers: an option, default exact fractions** (option C). `numbers: 'decimal'` prints terminating rationals as decimals; non-terminating ones stay fractions (§5.4).
4. **Assumptions: per variable, per call, real-only in v1** (option A). Every condition a rewrite needs, including caller assumptions it relied on, is returned with the result (§6).
5. **mathjs differences are corrected**, not matched. Each one is in the migration gate's `INTENDED` list with its reason (§15).
6. **π and e are symbolic coefficients** (option B). The coefficient domain is ℚ[π, e] (ℚ(π, e) for division), with π and e as independent transcendentals; this rests on the standard, unproven assumption that they are algebraically independent (§5.3). `e` alone or to an integer power is the constant; `e^u` otherwise is `exp(u)`. The solver gives exact roots for degree 1 and 2 and goes numeric, flagged inexact, for degree ≥ 3 (§8.3).
7. **`factor` is complete for univariate polynomials over ℚ** (option B), by Zassenhaus: content, square-free, Cantor–Zassenhaus mod p, Hensel lifting to the Landau–Mignotte bound, recombination under a budget; van Hoeij is the later speedup (§8.2). The scope that follows: multivariate input and ℚ[π, e] coefficients get content + square-free + gcd only, returned with `complete: false`; Wang's multivariate algorithm is out of v1.

Also recorded (author findings, 2026-10-15): `ln(exp(x)) → x` holds for every real x, and only `exp(ln(x)) → x` needs `x > 0` (§6). EQ's Newton step moves to `@arki/math/roots` `findRoots`, so EQ needs `polynomialIn` and `exactRoots`, not `derivative` (§15).
